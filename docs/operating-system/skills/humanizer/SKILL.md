# Humanizer — Unearth Self

Two jobs, in order:

1. Remove AI writing tells (patterns from Wikipedia’s “Signs of AI writing”, via [blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer)).
2. Land the result in the house voice in `03_BRAND_SYSTEM.md`.

Keep every supported fact. Do not add a number, name, date, or claim. If a sentence needs a detail you do not have, write a simpler sentence or ask.

## When to run

- New or rewritten guest-facing prose
- Journal drafts, campaign landers, suite copy, emails, one-pagers
- After any model (including this one) produces more than two sentences of copy

Do not run on: rate tables, legal clauses, code, rune names, hex values, partner names, open decisions.

## House voice (overrides the generic skill)

Confident, precise, slightly dry. Warm without being soft. Short sentences sit next to longer ones. We speak like people who have actually been outside.

Use: unearth, orient, notice, choose, carry, forge, imprint, pace.

Do not use: unleash your best self, trauma, triggered, activated, dysregulated, guru, shamanic (unless a named partner programme uses it with consent), grindset, curated experience, nestled, boasts, elevate, transformative, journey of self-discovery (public marketing), unlock your potential.

UK English in the OS. Canadian public site may follow D20; until then prefer UK spelling in new copy except where the live site already uses a Canadian form.

Four partners, no CEO. Do not write “our team of experts”, “the Unearth Self family”, or “as a company we believe”.

## How to work

1. **Mark the tells.** Strongest first. Paragraph shape counts: a contrast split across two sentences, three parallel examples, the same closer after every section.
2. **Draft.** Keep the information. Merge or split. Vary sentence length.
3. **Check aloud.** Hunt the five that survive a rewrite: not-X-but-Y, one-line closer, dash, triad, bold label.
4. **House pass.** Read it as if Tess or Lisa would send it. Cut anything they would not say on the property.

## Tells that always get an edit

### 1. Not X but Y
“It’s not a hotel, it’s a home.” “Stay with the land, not a programme.” State the thing. Keep a contrast only when the negative half corrects a belief the reader actually holds.

### 2. One-line closers
“That is the real work.” “Let that sink in.” “This is only the beginning.” If it restates, cut it. A short sentence that adds a fact can stay.

### 3. Staged run-up
“Here’s what you need to know.” “Let’s dive in.” “The thing is.” Delete the runway.

### 4. Forced triads
Three items only when there are three facts. “Kitchens, fireplaces, and a quiet stay” is a list. “Challenge, camaraderie, and transformation” is a costume.

### 5. Dashes as glue
No em dashes or en dashes in guest-facing copy unless a named writer’s sample uses them. Use a period, comma, or colon.

### 6. Inflated words
delve, tapestry, nestled, boasts, elevate, pivotal, robust (figurative), vibrant, showcase, testament, underscore, foster, landscape (abstract), journey (when it means “product”), unlock, curated, bespoke (except a real custom itinerary), world-class, premium experience.

### 7. Wellness slop (house-specific)
best self, lean in, hold space, container, medicine, sacred, download (emotional), activate, regulate your nervous system, as above: trauma/triggered/dysregulated unless a clinician is writing.

### 8. Fake authority
“Studies show.” “Experts agree.” Name the source or cut the claim.

## What good copy looks like here

> Four suites in a heritage house on 4 Street West. Kitchens, fireplaces, parking. Basecamp is next door when you want heat or a table.

> Water Mark has a custom Murphy and a small hidden room. King in the bedroom. The second bath has a jetted tub.

> Notice, don’t excavate.

Not:

> Nestled in the heart of Drumheller, Haven offers a curated selection of unique suites — each designed to elevate your stay and unlock a deeper connection with the land.
